How it works

Learn the good part. Flag everything else.

Defects are rare and varied, so collecting examples of every one is impractical. Anomaly detection turns the problem around: model normal parts precisely, and measure how far each new part strays from them.

1

Learn from good parts

A few hundred photos of good parts become a memory bank of small image patches, using features from a pretrained ResNet18.

2

Score every patch

Each patch of a new part is compared with its nearest good-part memory. Unusual patches light up the heatmap and raise the score.

3

Decide with three bands

Thresholds are fitted on a separate validation split: confident defects are rejected, uncertain parts go to a person.

Small defects on a tray of many capsules are only a few pixels wide at the 256 px model input, which is why Capsules scores lowest; a production build would tile the image or raise the resolution. Full ROC curves, confusion counts and latency are in the evaluation report.

Where it fits

Any line where “good” looks consistent.

Electronics assemblyBent pins, melted plastic, scratches and missing components on boards.
Pharma and packagingBubbles, discoloration, leaks and misshapen units across a tray.
Food gradingBurns, breakage, holes and spots on natural products that vary part to part.
